Safety

Preparing for pet emergencies

Planning before an emergency helps owners act quickly. Keep key records, transport arrangements and local veterinary contacts accessible.

Educational draft · Review before publication

Every household should know where to seek urgent veterinary help and how to transport its pet safely. Emergency plans should be adapted for local services, climate, transport and the needs of the animal.

Keep an emergency record

  • Veterinarian and after-hours clinic contact details.
  • Pet identification, microchip details if applicable, and a recent photograph.
  • Current medications, allergies and relevant medical history.
  • Carrier or lead, secure transport plan and any mobility needs.
  • Emergency supplies appropriate to your veterinarian's advice.

Know when not to wait

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, major bleeding, seizures, significant trauma, heatstroke signs or suspected poisoning require urgent veterinary contact. Call the clinic while arranging transport when possible. Do not delay urgent care to search online for a diagnosis.

Avoid unsafe home treatment

Do not induce vomiting or give human medicines unless a veterinarian or appropriate poison service specifically directs you to do so. Keep packaging or a photograph of any suspected substance and share the estimated amount and timing with the veterinary team.

Plan for heat and travel

Provide shade, ventilation and access to water, and never leave a pet unattended in a hot vehicle. Prepare carriers and transport plans in advance rather than during an emergency.

Important: This article is for general education and is not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a veterinarian for advice specific to your animal. Seek urgent local veterinary care if your pet has severe or rapidly worsening signs.
