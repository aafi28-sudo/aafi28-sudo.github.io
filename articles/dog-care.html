Dog care

Building a healthier everyday routine for your dog

Daily wellbeing is shaped by more than exercise alone. Nutrition, rest, social contact, enrichment, preventive veterinary care and a safe environment all contribute.

Educational draft · Review before publication

Dogs thrive when their daily routines meet their individual needs. Age, breed, health, body condition, temperament and environment all affect what is appropriate for a particular animal.

Start with the essentials

  • Nutrition and water: provide a complete, appropriate diet and constant access to clean drinking water unless your veterinarian has given specific instructions.
  • Movement: tailor walks and activity to the dog's age, fitness, health and ability. Increase activity gradually when appropriate.
  • Mental enrichment: provide safe opportunities to sniff, explore, learn, play and solve simple problems.
  • Rest and comfort: provide a quiet, comfortable resting place and protect sleep from repeated disturbance.
  • Preventive care: discuss vaccinations, parasite prevention, dental care, weight and routine examinations with your veterinarian.

Notice your dog's normal

Pay attention to usual appetite, drinking, energy, mobility, toileting, sleep and social behaviour. A short record can help you describe changes clearly. One unusual moment does not automatically mean disease, but persistent, repeated or worsening changes deserve attention.

Build a routine you can maintain

Choose a few achievable habits, such as a regular walk, a short enrichment activity, fresh water checks and a weekly look at body shape and coat. Adapt the routine as your dog ages or their needs change.

When to seek professional help

Contact a veterinarian when you notice a significant or persistent change, pain, repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, reduced mobility, unexplained weight change or another concerning sign. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, major bleeding or suspected poisoning needs urgent veterinary assistance.

Important: This article is for general education and is not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a veterinarian for advice specific to your animal. Seek urgent local veterinary care if your pet has severe or rapidly worsening signs.
