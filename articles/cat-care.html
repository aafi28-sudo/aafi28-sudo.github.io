Cat care

Understanding your cat's daily wellbeing

Cats can hide discomfort and may express stress through subtle changes in routine, movement, appetite or litter-box habits. Knowing what is normal for your cat helps you notice meaningful changes.

Educational draft · Review before publication

Cat wellbeing includes physical health, emotional security, opportunities for natural behaviour and an environment that provides choice and safety. Indoor and outdoor cats may have different risks, so care should be tailored to the individual animal.

Make the home work for your cat

  • Provide clean litter facilities in quiet, accessible locations.
  • Offer safe hiding places, elevated resting spots and undisturbed sleep.
  • Use appropriate play and feeding enrichment to encourage exploration and activity.
  • Keep fresh water available and provide a nutritionally complete diet suited to the cat's life stage.
  • Arrange preventive veterinary care, including dental and parasite discussions as appropriate.

Watch for changes

Record appetite, water intake, urination, stool, grooming, movement, hiding, vocalization and social interaction. A sudden change in litter-box habits or difficulty urinating can be urgent, especially in male cats; seek veterinary advice promptly.

Reduce avoidable stress

Predictable routines, gradual introductions to new people or animals, and access to resources can help. Avoid punishment for behaviour changes; first consider whether a medical problem, stressor or environmental issue could be involved.

When to contact a veterinarian

Seek advice for persistent appetite changes, weight loss, repeated vomiting, reduced grooming, pain, unusual hiding or changes in urination. Straining without producing urine, breathing difficulty, collapse or suspected toxin exposure requires urgent care.

Important: This article is for general education and is not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a veterinarian for advice specific to your animal. Seek urgent local veterinary care if your pet has severe or rapidly worsening signs.
